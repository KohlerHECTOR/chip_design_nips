%	1. LOGIC SYNTHESIS WITH AND-INVERTER GRAPHS
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\headerbox{1. Logic synthesis with AIGs}{name=introduction,column=0,row=0,span=1}{
Chip design needs the smallest circuit encoding a given Boolean function, which in practice means the smallest and-inverter graph (AIG): a DAG whose nodes are \textit{AND} gates and whose edge labels are \textit{NOT} signals.
Below are two functionally equivalent AIGs of $f(x_1, x_2, x_3) = (\overline{x_1}\land x_2,\ x_2 \land x_3)$ with different numbers of nodes; \texttt{ABC} primitives rewrite an AIG locally to reduce the \textit{AND} count while preserving equivalence~\citep{rewriting}.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
%	2. AIG REDUCTION AS AN MDP
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\headerbox{2. AIG reduction as an MDP}{name=how,column=1,row=0,span=2}{
Each action is one \texttt{ABC} primitive applied to the whole graph and the reward grows as the number of \textit{AND} nodes drops; since the primitives preserve functional equivalence, every state reachable from $\mathcal{G}_0$ still computes $f$.
Most reinforcement learning (RL) and other machine learning-based searches for logic synthesis act at this granularity~\citep{rl-gnn1,rl-logic1,flow-bandits}.
DAG generative models fail to generate functionally equivalent AIGs~\cite{graphbench}.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
%	5. WHY GRAPH REPRESENTATIONS DO NOT HELP
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\headerbox{5. Why don't graph representations help?}{name=optimal,column=0,below=ibmdp,span=2}{
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.43\linewidth}
Computing $V^\star$ exactly is out of reach, so we estimate it: a random flow of \texttt{ABC} primitives applied to a benchmark $\mathcal{G}_0$ gives a state $G_0^i$, whose target $\widehat{V}(G_0^i)$ is the smallest AND count reached by $M=100$ rollouts of eight further random primitives.
We build this supervised dataset in order to study the predictive power of message passing networks (1-WL) on AIGs with \texttt{Graphtester}~\citep{graphtester}.
\end{minipage}
\hfill

      act/.style={font=\tiny,inner sep=1.5pt},

%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
%	9. FUTURE WORK
%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\headerbox{8. Future work}{name=future,column=0,below=results,span=1,above=bottom}{
\begin{itemize}\compresslist
    \item Extend the analysis to non 1-WL representations, since some AIG-specific models might not be 1-WL.
    \item \texttt{ABC} implements many more than 5 primitives, but no search method uses them all. More actions would mean more varied states, which could be where graph representation learning helps.
\end{itemize}
